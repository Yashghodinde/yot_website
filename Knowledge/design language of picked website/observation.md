# Sahyadri Farms Website Observation

Reviewed on: 2026-06-04  
Primary reference: https://www.sahyadrifarms.com/

## Purpose of This Observation

The goal is to understand Sahyadri Farms' website deeply enough to create a similar but better-looking, better-filled agricultural value-chain website for Bhavarth. This document captures the design language, content strategy, page structure, brand signals, strengths, weaknesses, and recommendations for a stronger version.

## Pages Reviewed

- Homepage: https://www.sahyadrifarms.com/
- Our Story: https://www.sahyadrifarms.com/about-us/our-story
- Core Crops: https://www.sahyadrifarms.com/products/core-crops
- Food Safety Standards: https://www.sahyadrifarms.com/food-safety/standards
- Traceability: https://www.sahyadrifarms.com/food-safety/traceability

## High-Level Impression

Sahyadri Farms presents itself as a farmer-owned, large-scale, integrated fruits and vegetables value-chain company. The site is not trying to feel like a small organic farm brand. It is trying to feel like an agricultural institution: big, trusted, export-ready, technically capable, farmer-first, and backed by certifications.

The core brand message is:

- Large scale
- Farmer ownership
- Integrated value chain
- Traceability
- Food safety
- Infrastructure
- Global customers
- Sustainability and fair farmer value

The design supports this by using real farm imagery, green-heavy branding, certification logos, customer logos, numerical proof, and repeated references to technology and traceability.

## Brand Positioning

Sahyadri's positioning is built around trust and scale rather than lifestyle or premium retail appeal.

### Main Positioning Claims

- "India's Largest Integrated Fruits and Vegetables Value Chain"
- "A company of the farmers, by the farmers and for the farmers"
- Farmer-led initiative
- Farm-to-fork traceability
- Food safety and hygiene
- World-class infrastructure
- Global standards
- International customers

### Brand Personality

The site feels:

- Institutional
- Farmer-centric
- Practical
- Trust-building
- Export-oriented
- Proof-heavy
- Rural but modern
- More operational than emotional

It does not feel highly premium, editorial, luxury, minimal, or tech-startup polished. That creates an opportunity for our site: preserve the credibility but upgrade the experience.

### Navigation Observations

- The nav tries to expose almost everything from the top level.
- Dropdowns are hover-based and multi-level.
- Some items appear duplicated, such as PR & Media being both a direct nav item and a dropdown group.
- Some menu items are hidden in the markup but still part of the design system, suggesting the site has accumulated legacy sections.
- The structure is comprehensive but a little heavy for a first-time visitor.
- The top nav is more company-directory-like than conversion-focused.

### Recommendation for Bhavarth

Use fewer top-level nav items and group content more clearly:

- About
- Farmers
- Products
- Infrastructure
- Food Safety
- Sustainability
- Media
- Contact

For a more modern site, the nav should help users choose a journey quickly:

- Buyers should find products, quality standards, capacity, certifications, and inquiry forms.
- Farmers should find programs, benefits, support, and app/platform access.
- Investors and partners should find scale, governance, impact, and financial/institutional credibility.
- General visitors should understand the story and mission without opening deep menus.

## Homepage Structure

The homepage follows a trust-building sequence.

### 1. Header

The header uses:

- White/light background behavior over hero
- Left-aligned Sahyadri logo
- Dense horizontal navigation
- Hover dropdowns
- Simple text links
- No highly prominent primary CTA in the header

The header is functional but visually crowded. It gives the feeling of a Webflow-generated corporate site rather than a highly designed product experience.

### 2. Hero Section

The hero uses a full-screen background video of a lush grape farm. The poster frame shows rows of grapes and a green canopy.

Hero content:

- A short horizontal white bar
- Main heading: "India's Largest Integrated Fruits and Vegetables Value Chain"
- Supporting line: "A company of the farmers, by the farmers and for the farmers."
- CTA: "Explore"

Visual style:

- Full-bleed video
- Green agricultural imagery
- White or light text treatment
- Left-aligned text inside a centered container
- Large but not oversized heading
- Rounded green/yellow CTA button

What works:

- Immediate farm signal
- Real agricultural imagery
- Clear claim
- Good emotional connection to farmers

What can improve:

- The CTA "Explore" is generic.
- The headline is factual but not especially memorable.
- The hero could better communicate product categories, buyer value, export strength, or the Bhavarth mission.
- A stronger overlay system would improve contrast and polish.
- A secondary CTA could help split audiences, for example "Explore Products" and "Partner With Us".

### 3. Welcome and Statistics

The site uses a metrics section with icon/image cards:

- 18,000+ registered farmers
- 30,000+ acres of land
- 252+ villages covered
- 42+ countries served
- 110+ customers worldwide
- 12+ years in the industry

Design treatment:

- White cards
- Rounded corners
- Green icon blobs
- Large animated counters
- Short labels
- Grid layout

What works:

- The numbers quickly create credibility.
- The metrics support the "largest value chain" claim.
- The counter animation adds movement.

What can improve:

- The icon style is basic and somewhat dated.
- The metrics would be stronger with context: crop volume, export capacity, processing capacity, farmer income impact, traceability coverage, cold-chain size, certifications, or processing lines.
- For Bhavarth, the stats should feel like a boardroom proof strip: fewer numbers, stronger hierarchy, and sharper explanation.

### 4. About Sahyadri Farms

The about section explains:

- Sahyadri is an integrated platform for fruits and vegetables.
- It works with farmers through an inclusive partnership.
- It covers primary processing, semi-processed products, processed products, and waste processing.
- It aims to give small farmers fair compensation.
- It follows global agricultural practices and food safety standards.

Tone:

- Dense
- Institutional
- Mission-led
- Operational

What works:

- It clearly connects farmer welfare with global standards and infrastructure.
- It establishes seriousness.

What can improve:

- The paragraphs are long.
- There is limited visual hierarchy.
- The copy can be more human and easier to scan.
- It would benefit from a simple value-chain diagram or "from farm to market" flow.

### 5. Infrastructure and Technology

This section introduces:

- World-class infrastructure at Mohadi, Nashik
- State-of-the-art facilities
- Scale, safety, and quality
- Farmer monitoring
- SAP
- Real-time traceable supply-chain software
- Transparency for farmers and consumers

This is one of the strongest strategic sections because it moves the brand from "farmer story" to "operational capability."

Recommendation for Bhavarth:

Make this section visually richer. Use:

- Facility imagery
- Process timeline
- Capacity numbers
- Cold-chain and pack-house details
- Lab/quality workflow
- Traceability platform preview
- Map of farmer clusters and markets

### 6. Pillars of Sahyadri Farms

The homepage presents four pillars:

- Largest integrated value chain
- Farm-to-fork traceability
- Safety and hygiene
- Farmer-led initiative

Each pillar uses a simple icon and a short paragraph.

What works:

- The four-pillar structure is clear and reusable.
- It turns a complex organization into digestible themes.
- It balances customer trust and farmer value.

What can improve:

- Icons are very simple.
- Paragraphs are similar in tone and density.
- Visual cards could be more polished.
- Each pillar should have a stronger proof point or micro-interaction.

For Bhavarth, this can become a more premium "Operating Principles" section:

- Farmer Prosperity
- Integrated Supply Chain
- Verified Quality
- Climate-Smart Agriculture
- Transparent Markets

### 7. Licences and Certifications

The site shows a certification/logo section with logos for:

- FSSAI
- APEDA
- FSSC
- FSSC 22000
- BRCGS
- Global G.A.P.
- Fairtrade
- Tesco
- FDA and other certifications

The Food Safety page expands this into explanation blocks and certificate links.

What works:

- Strong trust builder for B2B, exports, retail buyers, and institutional partners.
- Showing actual certification logos makes the site more credible.

What can improve:

- The logo grid/slider can feel cluttered.
- Certifications should be grouped by purpose: food safety, export, sustainability, retail compliance.
- Each certification could have a short "why it matters" line.

### 8. Esteemed Customers

The homepage uses an autoplay logo slider for customer proof. Logos include domestic and international retail/food brands.

What works:

- Social proof is very strong.
- Logo slider suggests global reach.

What can improve:

- Sliders can hide important logos.
- A static, well-composed proof wall may feel more premium.
- The section could include customer categories: retailers, food brands, export partners, processors.

### 9. What's New

The site shows three news/initiative cards:

- Karta Shetkari
- Paani Foundation
- Agro Tourism YouTube embed

Design treatment:

- Three-column grid
- Image or video on top
- Text below
- Grey card background

What works:

- Shows that the organization is active beyond products.
- Adds social impact and community credibility.

What can improve:

- Cards feel visually inconsistent.
- The content is not clearly prioritized.
- News could be better presented as impact stories or field reports.

### 10. Footer

The footer includes:

- Registered office and factory address
- Phone and email
- Featured links
- Help links
- Farmer app download badges
- Enterprise login
- SetuVerified link
- Social links
- FarmSetu Supply Chain Stack credit
- A panoramic farm image band at the very bottom

What works:

- Gives practical contact information.
- Reinforces farmer app and technology ecosystem.
- The farm image band keeps the agrarian identity to the end.

What can improve:

- The footer is information-heavy.
- Link hierarchy can be clearer.
- The address block is dense.
- Contact CTA should be stronger and more direct.

## Visual Design Language

### Color Palette

Observed dominant colors:

- Fresh green: #6B9C2F
- Dark green: #006638
- Lime/harvest green: #AFC83B
- White: #FFFFFF
- Charcoal/dark text: #333333
- Black: #000000
- Light green tints: #EBFFF6, #EDF3E6, #F0FFDE
- Light greys: #F5F5F5, #F8F8F8, #E9E9E9, #DFDFDF

Color impression:

- Green is the central identity color.
- Lime green is used for buttons, headers, and accents.
- White backgrounds dominate content sections.
- Grey is used for card backgrounds and quiet section breaks.
- Occasional blue/teal appears in initiative graphics, not core brand.

Recommendation for Bhavarth:

Use a more sophisticated agricultural palette:

- Deep leaf green for trust and authority
- Fresh crop green for growth
- Warm harvest gold for highlights
- Clay/soil neutral for grounding
- Off-white for calm backgrounds
- Charcoal for readable text

Avoid making the whole site only green. Green should lead, but not flatten the experience.

### Typography

The site loads multiple Google font families:

- Lato
- Ubuntu
- Montserrat
- Inter
- Nunito

Actual visible usage appears heavily based on Nunito, with Lato and other fonts appearing in supporting components.

Typography impression:

- Friendly and rounded
- Readable
- Slightly informal
- Not very premium
- Too many font families in the source
- Headings and body copy can feel samey

Recommendation for Bhavarth:

Use fewer fonts:

- One strong, modern sans for headings
- One highly readable sans for body copy

Possible direction:

- Headings: Sora, Manrope, Plus Jakarta Sans, or Fraunces if a more editorial tone is desired
- Body: Inter, Source Sans 3, or Nunito Sans

Keep hierarchy sharper:

- Hero headline should be confident and high contrast
- Section headings should be compact and purposeful
- Body copy should use shorter line lengths
- Metrics should have strong numerals and small explanatory text

- Grape canopy video hero
- Farm rows

- Certification logos

### Iconography

Observed icon style:

- Simple flat icons
- White icon shapes on green organic blobs
- SVG line/flat icons for pillars
- Leaf icons as bullets
- Social icons in circular green buttons

Iconography is clear but basic.

Recommendation:

For Bhavarth, use a more refined icon system:

- Thin-line icons for UI and navigation
- Solid pictograms only where they support impact metrics
- Avoid mixed icon styles
- Use icons sparingly beside strong headings or proof points

### Shape Language

Observed:

- Rounded buttons
- Rounded stat cards
- Organic blob backgrounds behind metric icons
- Green rectangular section headers
- Some square/rectangular news cards
- Simple grid-based cards

The shape language is friendly but not fully consistent.

Recommendation:

Use a controlled system:

- 8px to 12px radius for cards
- Pill buttons only for primary actions
- Avoid excessive blob shapes unless brand art direction intentionally uses them
- Use clean grids, generous spacing, and subtle borders

- Webflow-generated CSS and JS
- Google WebFont loader
- Multiple font families
- Webflow slider components
- Background video component
- Counter script
- Hidden sections/components
- Recaptcha script
- Google analytics/tagging
- Several CDN-hosted images and PDFs

The source also shows the site was last published on Tue May 12 2026.

Performance concerns to keep in mind for our site:

- Hero videos can be heavy.
- Multiple sliders and large image assets can affect page speed.
- Too many font families increase load.
- Large logo/certificate assets should be optimized.
- Hidden legacy content should not ship in the final production build.

## Final Takeaway

Sahyadri Farms has strong content foundations: farmer ownership, scale, infrastructure, food safety, traceability, and global proof. Its weakness is presentation. The current site feels credible but visually dated, crowded, and uneven across sections.

For Bhavarth, the winning direction is not to copy the surface design. The better approach is to copy the strategic spine:

1. Start with a clear agricultural value-chain claim.
2. Prove scale early.
3. Humanize the farmer relationship.
4. Show infrastructure and technology.
5. Make food safety and traceability visible.
6. Use certifications and customers as trust proof.
7. Give every visitor a clear next step.

If we execute this with cleaner design, stronger copy, richer product information, and better visual hierarchy, Bhavarth can feel more modern and more trustworthy while still carrying the same agricultural credibility that makes Sahyadri effective.
